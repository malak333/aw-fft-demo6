Final Fantasy Tactics

A Tactical RPG for the PlayStation

Released: June 20, 1997 (Japan) • North America: January 28, 1998

Overview

Final Fantasy Tactics is a tactical role-playing game developed by Square for the Sony PlayStation. Square published the Japanese release on June 20, 1997, and Sony Computer Entertainment published the North American release on January 28, 1998. It stands as one of the most acclaimed entries in the long-running Final Fantasy franchise.

Set in the fantasy world of Ivalice, the game combines deep strategic combat with a rich political narrative. It was directed by Yasumi Matsuno, with character designs by Akihiko Yoshida and art direction by Hiroshi Minagawa. The game was praised for its complex storyline, challenging gameplay, and memorable soundtrack.

Story & Setting

The World of Ivalice

The game takes place in the fictional world of Ivalice, a land torn apart by religious conflict, political intrigue, and war. The continent is divided among several nations, each with its own agenda, and the player navigates through a web of alliances and betrayals.

The Ramza Story

The story follows two central characters: Delita Heiral, a commoner who becomes entangled in the affairs of the nobility, and Ramza Beoulve, the third son of House Beoulve (after his older brothers Dycedarg and Zalbag). Ramza discovers a conspiracy involving the Church of Glabados, the nobility, and the mysterious Order of the Northern Sky. As Ramza seeks to uncover the truth and protect those he cares about, Delita rises through the ranks of the military, ultimately shaping the fate of Ivalice in ways neither could have predicted.

Themes

The narrative explores themes of class conflict, religious corruption, the futility of war, and the struggle between individual morality and institutional power. The game is widely regarded as one of the most mature and sophisticated stories in the Final Fantasy series.

Gameplay

Tactical Combat

Final Fantasy Tactics features turn-based tactical combat on a square-tile grid battlefield. Players must consider terrain, elevation, line of sight, and elemental weaknesses when planning their moves. Each unit has a limited number of actions per turn, and positioning is critical to success.

  • Square-tile grid: Units move across a grid of square tiles, with terrain types (grass, mountains, water, etc.) affecting movement and defense.
  • Turn order: Actions are determined by the Spd (Speed) stat and the Charge Time system, where units must wait a number of turns before acting again.
  • Elemental system: Weapons and spells have elemental affinities (Fire, Ice, Lightning, Earth, Wind, Water, Holy, Dark) that affect damage and status effects.
  • Break system: Knights can use Battle Skill abilities such as Weapon Break and Armor Break to destroy equipped items. Elemental affinity affects damage; it does not cause equipment to break.

Story Progression

The game is divided into multiple chapters, each focusing on different characters and story arcs. Players complete missions to advance the plot, with optional objectives adding replay value.

The Job System

One of the most celebrated features of Final Fantasy Tactics is its flexible job system. Characters can learn abilities from multiple jobs and combine them to create powerful custom builds.

Job Classes

There are over 30 job classes in the game, spanning several categories:

  • Basic Jobs: Squire, Chemist, Knight, Archer, Monk, Priest, Wizard, Thief
  • Advanced Jobs: Lancer, Samurai, Ninja, Geomancer, Bard, Calculator, Mime
  • Unlockable Jobs: Time Mage, Dancer, Summoner, Oracle, Mediator
  • Character-Specific Jobs: Holy Knight (Agrias), Engineer (Mustadio), Soldier (Cloud)

Job Points (JP)

Characters earn Job Points (JP) by performing actions related to a specific job. JP is used to purchase abilities and spells from the job's ability list. Each ability has a JP cost, and characters can freely spend JP to unlock any ability their current job allows. Character stats grow through leveling and depend on jobs and equipment, not through JP purchases.

Job Advancement

Advancing to a new job class is a separate system from spending JP. Each job has prerequisite job levels that must be reached before it can be equipped. For example, to unlock the Lancer job, a character must first reach Thief job level 3. Some jobs require levels in multiple prerequisite jobs. Once the prerequisites are met, the job becomes available to equip, but no additional JP is spent to "unlock" the job itself—JP is only spent on individual abilities within each job.

Ability Learning

Characters purchase abilities using Job Points (JP). Each job has a set of abilities and spells that can be unlocked. Characters can learn abilities from multiple jobs, allowing for deep character customization and strategic depth. Character stats grow through leveling and depend on jobs and equipment, not through JP purchases.

Music & Soundtrack

The soundtrack was composed by Hitoshi Sakimoto, with additional music by Masaharu Iwata. The music is widely praised for its emotional depth and atmospheric quality.

Notable tracks include:

  • Trisection — A dramatic battle theme
  • Antipyretic — A somber, atmospheric track
  • Battle on the Bridge — An intense battle theme
  • Seeker — An adventurous exploration track
  • For Those Who Bleed — A dramatic battle theme

Legacy

Final Fantasy Tactics has had a lasting impact on the tactical RPG genre and the broader Final Fantasy franchise:

  • Spin-offs: The world of Ivalice was revisited in Final Fantasy XII, Vagrant Story, and Final Fantasy Tactics: The War of the Lions (a 2007 PSP remake).
  • Reception: The game received critical acclaim for its story, gameplay, and depth. It holds a Metacritic score of 83/100 for the original PlayStation release.
  • Influence: Its job system and tactical combat inspired numerous subsequent games in the genre.
  • Cult Following: Decades after its release, FFT remains a beloved title with an active fan community, speedrunning scene, and ongoing discussions about its narrative and mechanics.

The 2007 remake, Final Fantasy Tactics: The War of the Lions, added new content, voice acting, and updated graphics, introducing the game to a new generation of players. However, the original PlayStation version retains its unique charm and historical significance.
